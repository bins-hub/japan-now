Separate a temple visit from a shopping walk

How to read the view

Use the view to understand pedestrian movement and light in the area actually shown. Check whether the camera description identifies the gate or the main-hall area. One view cannot establish conditions throughout Nakamise or whether individual shops are open.

Plan your visit

  1. Find Kaminarimon from Asakusa Station and check the exit for the railway line you will use.
  2. Follow the Kaminarimon–Nakamise–temple route, allowing separately for worship and browsing shops.
  3. If adding the Sumida riverside, map that leg and your return station. Shibuya and Shinjuku require a separate journey.

Do people in an evening picture mean the shops are open?

Pedestrian activity and shop opening hours are different things. Check the temple's information for worship and each shop's information for shopping.

Beyond the camera: Sensoji Temple, Asakusa

A temple visit, Nakamise shopping and a Sumida River walk each follow different schedules.

WATCH · Read the view

Visitors in the temple precinct do not necessarily mean that Nakamise shops are open.

RUN · Move safely

Walk through the shopping and worship areas rather than treating them as a running route. Check riverside paths separately if you intend to run.

EAT · Choose locally

Explore snack stalls and restaurants as separate choices; verify each business's opening hours before going.

Three steps for your visit

  1. Set Kaminarimon Gate as your first point from Asakusa Station.
  2. Continue through Nakamise toward Sensoji while respecting the worship route.
  3. For a longer stroll, plan the riverfront as a separate stop and confirm any boat schedule.

What the camera cannot tell you: Temple access and shop trading hours are not the same.
