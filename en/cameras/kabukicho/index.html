Plan a Kabukicho evening around a named destination

How to read the view

Bright neon is not a reliable measure of daylight or the recording time: signs and camera exposure affect the picture. Identify the intersection or street named by the broadcaster before choosing a meeting point.

Plan your visit

  1. Distinguish Kabukicho on Shinjuku's east side from the high-rise district to the west.
  2. Save the building name, address and entrance for your destination rather than navigating by a similar-looking sign.
  3. Treat a visit to the metropolitan government area on the west side as another travel leg. Check your rail service's last departure if returning late.

Do bright signs and crowds mean every venue is open?

Lighting and foot traffic do not establish a venue's hours. For a meal or performance, check that venue's admission or reservation information.

Beyond the camera: Kabukicho, Shinjuku

Use the bright nighttime scene to pick a landmark, then check the walk and your return journey.

WATCH · Read the view

Neon lights and people in view cannot verify the safety, accessibility or business hours of side streets and indoor venues.

RUN · Move safely

The busy nightlife core is a walking destination, not a recommended running route.

EAT · Choose locally

Late-night eateries vary in last-order times and payment methods. Confirm those with the venue directly.

Three steps for your visit

  1. Distinguish JR Shinjuku East Exit from Seibu-Shinjuku Station.
  2. Set a specific landmark, such as the Toho building, rather than navigating by the camera alone.
  3. Check the route back to your accommodation and the last train before a late meal.

What the camera cannot tell you: The camera only shows part of the district; it does not establish conditions elsewhere.
