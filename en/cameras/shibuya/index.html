Use the crossing view to plan your next stop

How to read the view

A crowd stopping and moving together reflects the traffic-light cycle. Watch several pedestrian phases rather than judging one frame. Umbrellas and reflections can offer context, but the view does not show station gates or indoor queues.

Plan your visit

  1. Locate Hachiko and the crossing first; choose a named exit or building as your meeting point.
  2. Plan Center-gai as a separate shopping leg after the crossing, rather than assuming the camera covers its side streets.
  3. Check the admission information for any observation facility separately. A quiet street does not establish a short indoor queue.

Does a dry-looking picture mean I can leave my umbrella behind?

Rain can be difficult to see from a sheltered camera or against bright signs. Use the view for street context and check the destination forecast before setting out.

Beyond the camera: Shibuya Scramble Crossing

Treat the camera as a starting point for choosing a walking route, not a measure of how crowded every nearby venue is.

WATCH · Read the view

Pedestrians move in waves with the signals. An empty crossing between signals does not mean nearby shops are quiet.

RUN · Move safely

The crossing and Center Gai are best explored on foot. For a real run, choose a separately mapped route such as the Imperial Palace area and plan transport.

EAT · Choose locally

Pick an eatery in the area you are actually visiting. Check its entrance, opening hours and last orders directly.

Three steps for your visit

  1. Find Hachiko Exit and the statue before entering the crossing.
  2. Choose either the Center Gai shopping streets or a walk toward Miyashita Park.
  3. Check the return route and any attraction admission requirements before leaving.

What the camera cannot tell you: Station interiors, shop queues and attraction availability are not visible in this camera.
